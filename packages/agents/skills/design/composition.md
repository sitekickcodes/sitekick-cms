A page is a short story told in sections: an opening that says what this is, a middle that earns the reader's trust, and an ending that tells them what to do.

- Open with the hero: what this page is, in a heading of a few words, and one sentence under it. Only one hero per page, always first.
- Then the substance, most important first. Each section does one thing: explain, prove, show, answer. If two sections say the same thing, merge them.
- Close with the action: one call to action, at the end. A long page may have one more in the middle, never two in a row.
- Rhythm: alternate dense and light sections (text, then a picture; a grid, then a quote). Three text-heavy sections in a row is a wall.
- Surfaces: most sections sit on the light surface. Use soft to set a band apart, dark or accent for at most one or two moments of emphasis per page, never two in a row, and keep accent for the hero or the closing call to action.
- Length: most pages need four to seven sections. More than nine is usually two pages.
- Consistency: if the page already has a pattern (every section has an eyebrow, cards always have images), follow it.
